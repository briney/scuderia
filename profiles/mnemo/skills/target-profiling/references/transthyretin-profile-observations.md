# Transthyretin (TTR) profile observations (2026-08-16)

Twenty-sixth level-2 profile (approved tier, neuroscience/cardiovascular —
ATTR amyloidosis). TTR is the **second amyloid-targeting antibody profile**
(after Aβ) and the **first non-neurodegenerative amyloid target** — ATTR
amyloidosis deposits in peripheral nerves (ATTR-PN) and heart (ATTR-CM),
not the brain. TTR is also the **first profile where non-antibody therapies
dominate the approved landscape** (tafamidis, patisiran, inotersen,
vutrisiran) and antibodies are the emerging modality. 5 key papers
ingested: 4/5 PMC XML OA (Pharmaceuticals, Amyloid, Biology, Rev Cardiovasc
Med — all MDPI/open-access), 1/5 abstract-only (Neurochem Int — Elsevier,
not in PMC, jina returned only 2,388 chars, Wayback 429). 80% full-text
retrieval rate. ~49K chars (profile), 150 PMID citations across 5 unique
PMIDs. New observations:

## 1. Conformation-specific cryptotope approach — structure-based epitope selection

TTR is the first profile where the antibody discovery strategy is
explicitly **structure-based cryptotope selection**: comparing the native
tetramer structure against the monomer/fibril structure to identify
residues that are buried in the native state but exposed in the
pathological conformation. The Chakrabartty lab's approach (PMID 26981744,
PMID 39338387):

1. Compare tetramer vs. monomer structures segment by segment
2. Identify 5-10 residue segments that are buried in the tetramer
   (low % solvent accessibility) but freely accessible in the monomer
3. Raise antibodies against these peptide segments (as MAP or KLH
   conjugates)
4. Screen hybridomas for binding to misfolded TTR (pH4-TTR) but NOT
   native tetramer
5. Validate: fibril inhibition (substoichiometric), phagocytic clearance,
   and immunohistochemical staining of patient amyloid deposits

This is distinct from the Aβ approach (where epitopes are on a peptide,
not a folded protein, and conformational selectivity comes from
preferring aggregated vs. monomeric forms). For TTR, the conformational
selectivity comes from the **burial/exposure of the epitope itself**
across structural states.

For field 5 (epitope landscape) and field 11 (differentiation) of
amyloid-targeting profiles: (1) the cryptotope approach is generalizable
to any protein misfolding disease where native and pathological
conformations differ in surface accessibility; (2) epitope burial depth
in the native state is a quantifiable design parameter (e.g., 13%
accessible for TTR 89-97); (3) the cryo-EM fibril structure provides the
ground truth for epitope accessibility on the pathological form —
epitopes must be on the solvent-exposed periphery of the fibril. (PMID
39338387, PMID 26981744.)

## 2. Native protein sequestration — high circulating concentration as an antibody design constraint

TTR circulates at 3.6–7.2 µM (20–40 mg/dL) — a very high concentration
for a plasma protein. Even weak binding to the native tetramer can
sequester a significant fraction of the administered antibody, reducing
the effective dose available for amyloid clearance. This is a unique
constraint for amyloid-targeting antibodies that does not apply to Aβ
(Aβ monomer concentration in CSF is picomolar).

The design implication: **epitope burial depth in the native tetramer
is a critical selection criterion, not just conformational specificity.**
Antibodies targeting the F-strand cryptotope (89-97, 13% accessible) and
C-terminal (115-124) are deeply buried and minimize sequestration.
Antibodies targeting the unstructured loop (41-45, 39-44, 56-61) are
slightly more exposed and may face higher sequestration risk.

For field 6 (failure modes) of amyloid-targeting profiles where the
native protein is abundant in circulation: (1) quantify the native
protein concentration; (2) assess epitope accessibility in the native
state; (3) deeply buried epitopes are preferred; (4) even micromolar
binding to the native form at high concentration can reduce therapeutic
dose. This is analogous to the "peripheral sink" debate for anti-Aβ
antibodies, but more severe because TTR is 3-4 orders of magnitude
more concentrated than Aβ. (PMID 39338387.)

## 3. Dual mechanism of action — fibril inhibition + immune clearance

Anti-TTR depleter antibodies have a dual mechanism that is distinct
from both anti-Aβ antibodies and from non-antibody TTR therapies:

1. **Fibril inhibition** (substoichiometric): antibodies bind misfolded
   monomers and aggregates, preventing their contribution to fibril
   growth. Coramitug (14G8) inhibits at IC50 = 0.028 mg/mL; 5A1 at
   0.015 mg/mL. The antibody can also bind fibril ends, sealing off
   the polymerization interface. This is a direct biochemical effect
   requiring no immune system.

2. **Immune clearance** (Fc-mediated): antibody-opsonized monomers,
   oligomers, and fibrils are phagocytosed by macrophages and microglia
   via Fcγ receptors. This is the amyloid debulking mechanism.

This dual mechanism sets depleter antibodies apart from silencers
(reduce TTR production) and stabilizers (prevent tetramer dissociation),
which only prevent NEW amyloid formation and cannot remove pre-existing
deposits.

For field 2 (biological mechanism) and field 6 (success factors) of
amyloid-targeting profiles: (1) document both mechanisms separately;
(2) substoichiometric fibril inhibition indicates the antibody targets
low-abundance but high-impact nucleation species; (3) Fc-mediated
clearance requires IgG1 isotype (consistent with the Aβ lesson that
IgG1 is essential for plaque clearance); (4) the dual mechanism is the
key competitive advantage over non-antibody therapies. (PMID 39338387,
PMID 26981744, PMID 36290413.)

## 4. Natural antibody validation — spontaneous disease regression

Three ATTR-CM patients (ages 68, 76, 82) showed spontaneous reversion
to near-normal cardiac structure and function BEFORE any disease-
modifying treatment. High-titer polyclonal IgG anti-TTR antibodies
were detected in their serum, binding to ATTR amyloid deposits and
synthetic TTR fibrils. The therapeutic antibody Ab-A shares epitope
overlap with these natural protective antibodies (competitive
inhibition assay, PMID 41923745).

This is the strongest clinical evidence that antibody-mediated amyloid
clearance can REVERSE ATTR — it demonstrates the target's disease-
modifying potential in humans without any therapeutic intervention.
No analogous natural-antibody validation exists for Aβ (the Aβ
graveyard-to-success story was driven by antibody engineering, not
natural immunity).

For field 3 (disease evidence) and field 6 (success factors) of
amyloid-targeting profiles: (1) search for case reports of spontaneous
disease regression — these may reveal natural antibody-mediated
clearance; (2) if natural antibodies are found, epitope comparison
with therapeutic candidates provides translational validation; (3)
the existence of natural protective antibodies is the strongest
possible evidence that the target is disease-modifiable via antibody
clearance. (PMID 39338387, PMID 41923745.)

## 5. Non-antibody-dominated target validation — 4 approved non-antibody therapies

TTR is the first profile where the approved therapeutic landscape is
entirely non-antibody: tafamidis (tetramer stabilizer, 2011/2019),
patisiran (siRNA, 2018), inotersen (ASO, 2018), vutrisiran (siRNA,
2022). These validate the target clinically but address only NEW
amyloid formation — none removes pre-existing deposits. The antibody
approach is differentiated by its ability to clear existing amyloid.

For field 4 (antibody landscape) and field 10 (competitive landscape)
of targets where non-antibody therapies dominate: (1) the target IS
clinically validated by the non-antibody therapies; (2) the antibody
must differentiate by a mechanism the non-antibody cannot provide
(deposit removal vs. prevention); (3) the competitive landscape
includes non-antibody therapies as benchmarks — the antibody must
offer additive or superior benefit; (4) combination therapy (antibody
+ stabilizer/silencer) is a natural strategy addressing both
prevention and clearance simultaneously. (PMID 39338387, PMID 36290413.)

## 6. PubMed search strategy for emerging antibody approaches — review-focused queries miss key primary papers

The task-specified PubMed queries ("transthyretin AND antibody AND
amyloid AND review", "TTR AND ATTR AND antibody AND review") returned
mostly clinical cardiology/neurology reviews about ATTR treatment —
not the key anti-TTR antibody primary research papers. The most
relevant papers (PMID 39338387, 26981744, 36290413, 41923745) were
found by adding targeted queries:
- "transthyretin amyloid antibody clearance immunotherapy"
- "anti-transthyretin monoclonal antibody amyloidosis"
- "transthyretin tetramer stability amyloid fibril formation"

These non-review queries surfaced the structural biology papers
(Chakrabartty), the conformation-specific mAb generation paper
(Higaki/Prothena), the preclinical neuropathy validation (Fassler),
and the translational natural-antibody study (George).

For orchestrators dispatching profiling subagents for emerging
antibody approaches (where the antibody is NOT yet approved and
primary research papers may not carry "review" tags): (1) always
include non-review queries focusing on the antibody mechanism
("anti-[target] monoclonal antibody", "[target] amyloid antibody
clearance"); (2) review queries are useful for the biology/disease
context but miss the key antibody development papers; (3) the
structural biology paper (comparing native vs. pathological
conformation) is often the highest-value single paper for
amyloid-targeting antibody profiles. (PMID 39338387.)

## 7. Elsevier Neurochemistry International — full text not retrievable

PMID 35218869 (Ueda 2022, Neurochem Int) was the only abstract-only
paper. Elsevier journal, not in PMC, jina reader returned only 2,388
chars (paywall), Wayback Machine returned HTTP 429 (rate limited).
This confirms the known Elsevier hard block pattern. The abstract
(947 chars) was sufficient for TTR biology context but could not
ground mechanistic details in full text. For field 2 (biological
mechanism), the Chakrabartty 2024 review (full text, 31K chars)
provided more detailed TTR biology than the Ueda review, compensating
for the missing full text. (PMID 35218869.)

## 8. MDPI open-access journals — 4/5 full-text retrieval rate

Four of five selected papers were published in MDPI open-access
journals (Pharmaceuticals, Amyloid [Taylor & Francis OA], Biology,
Reviews in Cardiovascular Medicine) — all with PMCIDs and PMC XML
OA. This is the first profile session where the majority of papers
were from MDPI-family OA publishers, yielding an 80% full-text rate
(matching the Aβ profile's 80%). The structural biology/comparative
review paper (Chakrabartty 2024, Pharmaceuticals, 31K chars) was
the single highest-value paper, providing: the TTR misfolding
pathway, all five depleter antibody epitopes mapped on cryo-EM
fibril structures, both clinical trial summaries, and the natural
antibody case reports. For orchestrators: when the target's key
researchers publish in OA-friendly journals (MDPI, Frontiers,
Nature Communications), full-text retrieval rates are high and the
profile can be richly grounded. (PMID 39338387, 26981744, 36290413,
41923745.)
