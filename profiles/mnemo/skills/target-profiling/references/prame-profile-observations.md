## Key new patterns

### 1. Intracellular cancer-testis antigen (CTA) — first non-surface target class

PRAME is the **first intracellular target profiled** in the entire profile
corpus. All prior targets are surface proteins, soluble proteins, or
membrane receptors — accessible to conventional antibody approaches. PRAME
is an intracellular nuclear/cytoplasmic protein that **cannot be targeted by
traditional antibodies**. The entire therapeutic strategy hinges on targeting
PRAME-derived peptides presented on MHC class I (pMHC) via:

- **TCR mimic (TCRm) antibodies** — antibodies that recognize pMHC complexes
  like a TCR (Pr20/Pr20M, PMID 28628042; PRAME425 TCE, PMID 40994043)
- **TCR-engineered T cell therapy (TCR-T)** — autologous T cells transduced
  with affinity-enhanced TCRs (IMA203, PMID 40205198; MDG1011, PMID 41008812)
- **TCR-CD3 bispecifics (ImmTAC)** — engineered TCR fused to anti-CD3
  engaging domain (PMID 39659431)
- **Protein/peptide vaccines** — recombinant PRAME protein + adjuvant
  (PMID 27843625)

**Implication for profiling**: When the target is intracellular, fields 4
(antibody landscape), 5 (epitope landscape), and 9 (structural information)
must be reframed:
- Field 4: "Antibody landscape" becomes "Therapeutic landscape" — the
  entries are TCRm antibodies, TCR-T therapies, TCR bispecifics, and
  vaccines, not just conventional antibodies. The "format" and
  "isotype/subclass" fields need adaptation (e.g., "TCR-T cell therapy,
  N/A isotype").
- Field 5: Epitopes are pMHC complexes (peptide + HLA allele), not surface
  protein domains. The "epitope" is defined by the peptide sequence, the HLA
  restriction (e.g., HLA-A*02:01), and the pMHC copy number per cell.
- Field 9: Structural information covers pMHC-TCRm/TCR complex structures
  (e.g., cryo-EM of HU-38 Fab/PRAME425/HLA-A*02:01 at 2.72 Å), not
  antibody-target protein complexes.

Generalizable: for any intracellular CTA target (NY-ESO-1, MAGE-A family,
WT1, etc.), the profile must lead with the intracellular/pMHC targeting
concept in fields 1, 2, 4, 5, and 9.

### 2. Proteasome-dependent epitope generation — a new biological dimension

The ALY peptide (PRAME 300–309) is generated by the **immunoproteasome** but
destroyed by the **constitutive proteasome** (PMID 28628042). The constitutive
proteasome cleaves internally within the ALY sequence (destructive
cleavage), while the immunoproteasome (specifically the β5i subunit)
generates the intact epitope. This means:

- **PRAME+ melanoma cells do not present the ALY peptide** under baseline
  conditions — Pr20 did not bind PRAME+ HLA-A2+ melanoma cell lines despite
  high PRAME expression.
- **IFN-γ treatment dramatically increases pMHC density** by upregulating
  immunoproteasome subunits, enabling Pr20 binding and ADCC.
- **Combination with checkpoint inhibitors is mechanistically rational** —
  IFN-γ from activated T cells can enhance immunoproteasome expression and
  pMHC presentation.

This is a unique biological dimension not seen in any prior profile. For
field 2 (biological mechanism), proteasome dependency should be noted as
it determines epitope availability. For field 6 (success factors/failure
modes), proteasome-dependent epitope generation is both a vulnerability
(low baseline pMHC density in cold tumors) and a combination opportunity
(IFN-γ–inducing agents enhance targeting).

Generalizable: for any intracellular antigen targeted via pMHC, document
which proteasome form generates the epitope and whether IFN-γ or
immunoproteasome inducers enhance pMHC density.

### 3. HLA restriction as a patient eligibility gate

All current PRAME therapies require HLA-A*02:01, the most common HLA allele
in Caucasians (~45%). This creates a hard patient eligibility gate —
patients without HLA-A*02:01 cannot receive any current PRAME-targeted
therapy. This is analogous to (but more restrictive than) biomarker
selection in conventional antibody therapy:

- HLA typing is a **germline genetic test** (not a tumor biomarker) — it
  cannot change with disease evolution.
- HLA loss-of-heterozygosity (LOH) in tumors is an immune escape mechanism
  that can eliminate HLA-A*02:01 expression even in eligible patients.
- HLA-A*24–restricted PRAME peptides have been described but not clinically
  pursued, limiting addressable population.

For field 4 (antibody/therapeutic landscape), note HLA restriction for each
therapy. For field 6 (failure modes), HLA LOH is a tumor escape mechanism
unique to HLA-restricted approaches. For field 10 (competitive landscape),
the HLA restriction is a barrier to entry but also limits market size.

Generalizable: for any pMHC-targeted therapy (TCRm, TCR-T, ImmTAC),
document the HLA restriction and its population prevalence, plus HLA LOH
as an escape mechanism.

### 4. Protein vaccine CD8+ T cell failure — a CTA-specific pattern

The GSK PRAME vaccine (recPRAME + AS15 immunostimulant) induced robust
humoral (antibody) and CD4+ T cell responses in all 66 treated patients but
**completely failed to elicit CD8+ cytotoxic T cell responses** (PMID
27843625). This is the effector mechanism required for tumor killing.

This failure is not vaccine-specific — it reflects the extremely low
circulating CD8+ precursor frequency for cancer-testis antigens (CTAs).
Unlike viral antigens (high precursor frequency from prior exposure), CTAs
are self-antigens with minimal prior T cell priming. The CD4+ response
alone may contribute to anti-tumor immunity via cytokine-mediated
senescence, but cannot replace cytotoxic killing.

Generalizable: for CTA targets, protein vaccines are unlikely to generate
CD8+ T cell responses. The recommended approaches are TCR-T (ex vivo
expansion of T cells), TCE/ImmTAC (bypassing the need for endogenous T
cell priming), or peptide/RNA vaccines (potentially better at cross-
presentation). Note this as a failure mode in field 6 for any CTA
target where a protein vaccine was attempted.

### 5. Low pMHC copy number sufficiency for T cell–engaging therapies

Key quantitative data on pMHC density thresholds:
- **ImmTAC bispecific**: as few as 10 pMHC epitopes per cell sufficient for
  T cell–mediated killing (PMID 39659431)
- **IMA203 TCR-T**: threshold ~40–50 peptide copies/cell (PMID 40205198)
- **PRAME425 TCE**: ~100 copies/cell (high) → ~10-fold more potent killing
  than ~20 copies/cell (low) (PMID 40994043)

These thresholds are dramatically lower than typical antibody-target binding
site requirements (>20,000 for B7-H3 ADC, >10,000 for many surface
antibody targets). The high sensitivity of TCR-based approaches to low pMHC
density is a key advantage over conventional antibodies for intracellular
targets.

For field 6 (success factors), the low copy number threshold is a
fundamental advantage of TCR-based approaches. For field 11
(differentiation), higher-affinity TCRm/TCR binders can target tumors with
lower pMHC density, expanding the addressable patient population.

Generalizable: for pMHC-targeted therapies, document the minimum pMHC
copy number required for activity and compare across modalities (TCRm,
TCR-T, ImmTAC, TCE).

HGNC ID, gene name, chromosome location, gene group membership, and NCBI
