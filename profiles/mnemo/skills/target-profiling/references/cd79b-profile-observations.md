# CD79b profile observations (2026-08-16)

Twenty-third level-2 profile (approved tier, oncology/hematological
malignancies — DLBCL, follicular lymphoma). CD79b (Igβ) is one of two
transmembrane signaling subunits (with CD79a/Igα) of the B-cell antigen
receptor (BCR) complex. Polatuzumab vedotin (Polivy™) — anti-CD79b MMAE
ADC — is the sole approved anti-CD79b antibody, approved for R/R DLBCL
(2019, pola-BR) and first-line DLBCL (2022, pola-R-CHP/POLARIX). 5 key
papers ingested, 5 new paper pages written. ~31K chars, 5 PMIDs cited.
Full-text retrieval: 4/5 PMC XML (80%), 1/5 abstract-only (Elsevier/Lancet
Haematology, no PMCID). New observations:

## 1. Rapid internalization is the target's primary value — the "internalization kinetics" success factor

CD79b's selection as an ADC target is driven by its rapid internalization
upon antibody binding, mediated by an AP2-binding YxxØ endocytosis motif
at Y196 in the cytoplasmic tail. This is mechanistically distinct from CD20
(which internalizes slowly) and is the reason CD79b was chosen over CD22
(pinatuzumab vedotin, anti-CD22 ADC) in the ROMULUS head-to-head — despite
comparable ORR, polatuzumab had longer duration of response and better
safety (0% vs 21% grade 5 AEs in DLBCL). For field 6 (success factors) of
ADC target profiles, **internalization kinetics** is a first-class success
factor distinct from expression level, epitope accessibility, or payload:
the rate of receptor-mediated endocytosis directly determines drug delivery
efficiency and is the primary differentiator between equally-expressed
surface targets. When profiling ADC targets, explicitly state the
internalization mechanism (clathrin-mediated, caveolin, etc.) and rate
relative to alternatives. (PMID 38203179, PMID 30935953, PMID 31352604.)

## 2. BCR biology review as the highest-value paper for ADC target profiles

The Tkachenko 2023 review (PMID 38203179, Int J Mol Sci, open access) was
the single most valuable paper for this profile — 312 CD79b mentions,
covering structure, ITAM signaling, internalization mechanism, expression
pattern, posttranslational modifications, disease associations, and
therapeutic implications. It filled fields 2 (biological mechanism), 3
(disease evidence — mechanistic), and 6 (success factors — internalization)
most richly. This confirms and extends the C5 observation ("structural
papers are the highest-value papers") and the IL-17A observation
("preclinical antibody characterization paper is the highest-value"):
for ADC targets, a comprehensive target biology review is the
equivalent of the structural paper for complement targets or the
preclinical characterization paper for naked-antibody targets. When
selecting 3-5 landmark papers for an ADC target profile, always include
the most recent comprehensive target biology review (preferably open
access for full-text retrieval). (PMID 38203179.)

## 3. Drug approval review (Deeks/Drugs) as a high-yield full-text source

The Deeks 2019 drug approval review (PMID 31352604, Drugs, open access
via PMC) provided the ADC composition (humanized IgG1-kappa, MMAE,
valine-citrulline cleavable linker, DAR ~3-4), mechanism of action,
pharmacodynamics (active regardless of ABC/GCL subtype and CD79B
mutation status), pharmacokinetics, dosing (1.8 mg/kg q21d), and safety
profile — filling fields 4 (antibody landscape), 7 (assay systems), and
8 (safety) in a single source. Springer/Adis drug-approval reviews
(Drugs journal) are consistently open access via PMC and provide
regulatory-grade drug summaries. This extends the DLL3 observation
(Springer/Adis retrieved via publisher-jina) with the note that when
the drug review IS in PMC OA, it is the most efficient single source
for fields 4, 7, and 8. Always include the drug approval review in the
paper set for approved-tier target profiles. (PMID 31352604.)

## 4. Pivotal trial + Phase 3 confirmation as the two-paper clinical evidence set

The Sehn 2020 pivotal Phase Ib/II (PMID 31693429, JCO, PMC OA) and Tilly
2022 Phase 3 POLARIX (PMID 34904799, NEJM, in PMC) together provided
complete clinical evidence for fields 3 (disease evidence) and 6 (success
factors). Both were retrievable via PMC XML (JCO is PMC OA; NEJM
POLARIX has a PMCID). The pivotal trial (Sehn) provided the approval-grade
efficacy/safety data (CR 40% vs 17.5%, OS 12.4 vs 4.7 months) plus the
CD79b expression biomarker data (96.4% IHC-positive, no expression-response
correlation). The Phase 3 (Tilly) provided the first-line confirmation
(PFS HR 0.73, safety comparable to R-CHOP). For approved-tier oncology
profiles, the two-paper set (pivotal trial + Phase 3 confirmation) is
sufficient for fields 3 and 6 when both are in PMC. (PMID 31693429,
PMID 34904799.)

## 5. Head-to-head ADC comparison (ROMULUS) as target selection evidence

The ROMULUS trial (PMID 30935953, Lancet Haematol, Elsevier paywall —
abstract-only) provided the only direct head-to-head comparison of CD79b
vs CD22 as ADC targets, using the same MMAE payload and linker. Despite
comparable ORR, CD79b (polatuzumab) was selected over CD22 (pinatuzumab)
for longer DOR and better safety (0% vs 21% grade 5 AEs in DLBCL). This
is unique evidence for field 6 (target selection rationale) and field 11
(differentiation — the internalization kinetics difference between CD79b
and CD22 likely explains the DOR advantage). Note: the Elsevier/Lancet
Haematology paywall blocked full-text retrieval (no PMCID, EPMC
hasPDF=N, jina returned only cookie consent pages). The 3,139-char
structured abstract was sufficient for distillation — confirming the
C5 observation that rich EPMC abstracts compensate for missing full text.
(PMID 30935953.)

## 6. CD79b downregulation as a target-specific resistance mechanism

The Tkachenko review notes that CD79b downregulation has been observed in
refractory DLBCL, suggesting that polatuzumab vedotin "might not be best
suited in this clinical situation." This is a target-specific resistance
mechanism (loss of surface target → reduced ADC binding/internalization)
distinct from payload-mediated resistance (e.g., MMAE transporter
upregulation). For field 6 (failure modes) of ADC target profiles,
explicitly distinguish: (a) target-loss resistance (downregulation,
mutation affecting epitope, internalization defect), (b) payload
resistance (drug efflux, target protein dysregulation), and (c) payload
toxicity-limiting dose (neuropathy preventing adequate dosing). CD79b
exhibits all three potential resistance modes. (PMID 38203179.)

## 7. The vincristine-to-polatuzumab substitution as a trial design success factor

POLARIX substituted vincristine in R-CHOP with polatuzumab vedotin
(pola-R-CHP) rather than adding polatuzumab on top of full R-CHOP. The
rationale: vincristine and MMAE both cause peripheral neuropathy, so
co-administration would produce additive neurotoxicity. By substituting
rather than adding, POLARIX achieved PN rates comparable to R-CHOP
(52.9% vs 53.9% any grade) — a critical design decision that made the
trial tolerable. For field 6 (success factors) of ADC target profiles
where the payload shares toxicity with standard-of-care chemotherapy,
the substitution design (replacing the toxic chemo component with the
ADC) is a validated trial design pattern. (PMID 34904799.)
