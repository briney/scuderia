# Specific Aims — requirement and drafting defaults

Load when drafting or checking a Specific Aims page — and only then; other
sections have their own guides (`section-structure.md` indexes them). The
argument-level criteria for this page live in `section-style.md`; the
mechanism's page budget in `nih-r01.md` / `nih-r21.md`.

## The NIH requirement

The Specific Aims attachment is required unless the NOFO specifies
otherwise, and follows the NIH Table of Page Limits — one page where the
mechanism's form set uses the Aims attachment; confirm the live table for
the specific mechanism. The application guide (PHS 398 Research Plan Form,
G.400 §2) asks the page to:

- State concisely the goals of the proposed research and summarize the
  expected outcome(s), including the impact the results will have on the
  research field(s) involved.
- List succinctly the specific objectives of the research proposed — to test
  a stated hypothesis, create a novel design, solve a specific problem,
  challenge an existing paradigm or clinical practice, address a critical
  barrier to progress in the field, or develop new technology.

Hyperlinks and URLs are not allowed in this section unless the NOFO permits
them.

## Structural defaults (craft, not mandate)

NIH does not mandate paragraph counts, aim counts, or sentence ranges for
this page. These are the cluster's working defaults; the author's own
conventions and an instance calibration note (below) override them:

- Open with the problem — the context, the gap, why current approaches fall
  short — before any methods or aims.
- State the overarching goal or central hypothesis, then transition to the
  aims. A combined goal-plus-transition paragraph is compact; splitting or
  deferring the goal is legitimate when the argument needs the space.
- One compact entry per aim, with consistent aim headers
  (`SPECIFIC AIM N: …`) so the page is scannable and each aim title stands
  alone. Sub-aims are acceptable when one aim genuinely groups distinct
  activities; they read as over-scoped when used to smuggle in extra aims.
- Close by naming what the project produces — the concrete deliverable, not
  a vague promise of impact. The "so what" a reviewer can weigh.
- Stay inside the one-page budget; character counts do not guarantee page
  fit — check the rendered page.

Aim count is a scoping decision (scientific scope, award duration,
mechanism), not a fixed convention. Do not manufacture a hypothesis for an
aim whose job is a resource, tool, or methodological deliverable.

## What stays out of this page

Approach-level detail — reagents, platforms, dosing, sort strategies — and
feasibility fallbacks belong in the Approach section. Fallback language at
the aim level plants feasibility doubt at the moment the page is earning
confidence. See `approach.md`.

## Instance calibration (optional)

If the instance's `AGENTS.md` declares a grant-writing calibration index,
load its Specific Aims note before drafting; it carries that author's
structural preferences and reviewer-context pointers. With no calibration
bound, these generic defaults apply — do not present them as any particular
author's voice.
