# Significance — requirement and drafting defaults

Load when drafting or checking the Significance section of the Research
Strategy — and only then. `section-structure.md` indexes the other section
guides; `nih-r01.md` / `nih-r21.md` carry the page budget and review
framework.

## The NIH requirement

Significance is one of the three Research Strategy sections (Significance,
Innovation, Approach), each with its required heading. The application guide
(PHS 398 Research Plan Form, G.400 §3.1) asks the section to:

- Explain the importance of the problem or critical barrier to progress
  that the proposed project addresses.
- Describe the strengths and weaknesses in the rigor of the prior research
  (both published and unpublished) that serves as the key support for the
  proposed project.
- Explain how the proposed project will improve scientific knowledge,
  technical capability, and/or clinical practice in one or more broad
  fields.

For research applications, also explain how the concepts, methods, technologies,
treatments, services, or preventative interventions in the field would change
if the aims are achieved; this is content, not a mandated closing paragraph.
Where an application has
multiple specific aims, the Significance, Innovation, and Approach
subsections may be addressed per aim or collectively — the author's choice.

The Significance and Innovation sections share the Research Strategy page
budget with Approach: 12 pages for an R01, 6 for an R21 (NIH Table of Page
Limits). The division between the three sections is the author's, not a
fixed allocation.

<https://grants.nih.gov/grants/how-to-apply-application-guide/forms-i/general/g.400-phs-398-research-plan-form.htm>

## Where rigor content actually lives

The current application guide places **rigor of the prior research** in
Significance (strengths *and* weaknesses — including unpublished work) but
places the **experimental design** rigor — robust and unbiased methods,
data collection and analysis — and **relevant biological variables** (e.g.
sex as a biological variable, with justification for single-sex studies)
in the Approach section. Do not treat a fixed set of Significance sublabels
("Investigators", "Scientific rigor", "Preliminary results") as NIH
mandates; current instructions govern content and placement. Preliminary
data belongs in the Research Strategy (Approach, or integrated into the
main sections) and its expectation is mechanism-specific — see `approach.md`
and the mechanism files.

## Structural defaults (craft, not mandate)

- Open with the problem: the disease, system, or computational context, its
  burden or cost, and the inadequacy of existing countermeasures.
- Name the specific gap explicitly — what is not known or cannot be done,
  and what the field would gain from filling it. The gap is not just "X is
  missing" but "without X we cannot Y, and Y matters."
- Survey the prior art with descriptive bold-label topic headers, one
  paragraph per topic, so the landscape is scannable. Label wording is the
  author's choice; the labeled-topic survey is a craft device, not a
  requirement.
- Close by turning the gap into the case for this proposal — the transition
  the Approach picks up. A brief preliminary-results bridge to Approach is
  optional and should not duplicate the full evidence presented later.
- Pitch every claim at the review factor this section scores under
  ("Importance of the Research" in the 2025 simplified framework — see
  `nih-r01.md`).

## Instance calibration (optional)

If the instance's `AGENTS.md` declares a grant-writing calibration index,
load its Significance note before drafting; it carries that author's
structural preferences. With no calibration bound, these generic defaults
apply — do not present them as any particular author's voice.
