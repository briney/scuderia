# Project Summary / Abstract — requirement and drafting defaults

Load when drafting or checking the Project Summary (Abstract) — and only
then. `section-structure.md` indexes the other section guides.

## The NIH requirement

The Project Summary/Abstract attachment (G.220 §7) is required. Per the
application guide:

- **30 lines of text**, following font and margin specifications; a summary
  that exceeds the limit is flagged as an error at submission. The NOFO can
  impose its own limit.
- A succinct and accurate description of the proposed work, **able to stand
  on its own** separate from the application.
- Informative to other persons working in the same or related fields, and
  understandable to a **scientifically literate reader** (not necessarily a
  specialist in this subfield).
- **Avoid descriptions of past accomplishments and the first person.**
- Content: state the application's broad, long-term objectives and specific
  aims, referencing the health relatedness of the project; describe the
  research design and methods for achieving the goals. The summary must
  reflect the key focus so the application is categorized correctly.
- **No proprietary, confidential information or trade secrets.** If funded,
  the summary becomes public (RePORT).
- No hyperlinks or URLs unless the NOFO allows them.

<https://grants.nih.gov/grants/how-to-apply-application-guide/forms-i/general/g.220-r&r-other-project-information-form.htm>

## Structural defaults (craft, not mandate)

- **Paragraph 1 — background and problem.** The problem, its significance,
  and the gap, defined for a science-literate reader outside the immediate
  specialty. Define terms; avoid subfield jargon.
- **Paragraph 2 — approach and aims.** What the proposal will do and how.
  Two legitimate forms: an inline aim list (aim titles stated, no aim-body
  text — scannable) or continuous narrative (aims referenced narratively —
  readable). Choose by whether the aim titles stand alone.
- **Optional closing** on public-health relevance in plain language, within
  the 30-line budget. Do not paste the Project Narrative here as a closing
  paragraph; they are different documents with different audiences — see
  `project-narrative.md`.

A short summary has no room for nuance: the background and the approach
must tell one self-consistent story. If the opening says "we have X" and the
approach says "we need X," the reader sees a contradiction even when the
distinction is real — resolve it in the summary's own terms.

## Instance calibration (optional)

If the instance's `AGENTS.md` declares a grant-writing calibration index,
load its Project Summary note before drafting; it carries that author's
structural preferences. With no calibration bound, these generic defaults
apply — do not present them as any particular author's voice.
