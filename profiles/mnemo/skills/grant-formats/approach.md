# Approach — requirement and drafting defaults

Load when drafting or checking the Approach section of the Research
Strategy — and only then. `section-structure.md` indexes the other section
guides; `nih-r01.md` / `nih-r21.md` carry the page budget, preliminary-data
expectation, and review framework.

## The NIH requirement

Approach is the third Research Strategy heading and usually the largest.
The application guide (PHS 398 Research Plan Form, G.400 §3.3) asks it to:

- Describe the overall strategy, methodology, and analyses to accomplish
  the specific aims.
- Describe plans to address weaknesses in the rigor of the prior research
  that serves as key support for the project (the counterpart of the
  Significance section's strengths/weaknesses review).
- Describe the experimental design and methods, and how they will achieve
  **robust and unbiased results** — including how data will be collected,
  analyzed, and interpreted.
- Discuss **potential problems, alternative strategies, and benchmarks for
  success**. Early-stage projects describe how feasibility will be
  established and how high-risk aspects are managed.
- Explain how **relevant biological variables** (such as sex, for
  vertebrate animal and human studies) are factored into research designs
  and analyses; single-sex studies need strong justification from the
  literature, preliminary data, or other relevant considerations.

Where an application has multiple specific aims, Significance / Innovation
/ Approach may be addressed per aim or collectively. Discuss pertinent
preliminary studies, data, or experience as applicable; preliminary data may
be integrated into the Research Strategy rather than placed under a fixed
heading. Mechanism-specific expectations differ: R21 preliminary data are not
required, while R01 feasibility generally depends more heavily on supporting
evidence. Neither a heading nor a generic cross-system demonstration proves
feasibility. See the mechanism file and current NOFO; an exploratory project
still needs a credible design and a plan to establish feasibility.

## Structural defaults (craft, not mandate)

- Reuse the Specific Aims page's aim titles as the Approach's aim headers,
  so the reviewer tracks the same objects throughout.
- Within each aim, the recurring labeled-subsection sequence (order is a
  working default, not a mandate):
  1. **Rationale and preliminary data** — the scientific basis and the
     feasibility evidence *for the system being proposed*; preliminary data
     from other systems does not automatically transfer.
  2. **Experimental approach** — the plan, organized by activity with
     descriptive bold-label headers as needed. Be specific enough that a reviewer can judge
     feasibility: platforms, parameters, controls, sample sizes.
  3. **Expected outcomes / results** — what the aim produces, named
     concretely (datasets with scale, models, validated tools,
     structures with resolution targets).
  4. **Potential problems and alternative approaches** — name the specific
     risks of *this* aim with a concrete alternative for each; a generic
     "we have extensive experience" paragraph earns nothing. Place this
     subsection where the risk actually lives — per aim, or on the riskiest
     aim — rather than mechanically on every aim.
- Aim independence: each aim should have its own rationale,
  feasibility basis, and useful deliverable; where a genuine dependency exists,
  name it and address its risk in the pitfalls rather than hiding it.
- Compression is legitimate when the rationale or evidence is supplied
  elsewhere in the application (e.g. a thorough Significance section);
  omission by compression is a choice to defend, not an oversight.

## Instance calibration (optional)

If the instance's `AGENTS.md` declares a grant-writing calibration index,
load its Approach note before drafting; it carries that author's structural
preferences. With no calibration bound, these generic defaults apply — do
not present them as any particular author's voice.
