# Innovation — requirement and drafting defaults

Load when drafting or checking the Innovation section of the Research
Strategy — and only then. `section-structure.md` indexes the other section
guides; `nih-r01.md` / `nih-r21.md` carry the page budget and review
framework.

## The NIH requirement

Innovation is the second Research Strategy heading. The application guide
(PHS 398 Research Plan Form, G.400 §3.2) asks the section to:

- Explain how the application challenges and seeks to shift current
  research or clinical practice paradigms.
- Describe any novel theoretical concepts, approaches or methodologies,
  instrumentation or interventions to be developed or used, and any
  advantage over existing methodologies, instrumentation, or interventions.
- Explain any refinements, improvements, or new applications of theoretical
  concepts, approaches or methodologies, instrumentation, or interventions.

Innovation shares the Research Strategy page budget with Significance and
Approach (12 pages R01, 6 R21) and is usually the shortest of the three —
sometimes a single paragraph on smaller mechanisms. The NOFO governs any
mechanism-specific innovation emphasis (an R21 in particular is reviewed
for the novelty and potential of the idea — see `nih-r21.md`).

<https://grants.nih.gov/grants/how-to-apply-application-guide/forms-i/general/g.400-phs-398-research-plan-form.htm>

## Structural defaults (craft, not mandate)

- Give each distinct innovation its own paragraph, with a descriptive
  bold-label header naming what it is. One paragraph, one innovation —
  mixing two innovations in one paragraph buries both.
- Inside each paragraph: name the innovation, say what it is and what is
  new against the relevant comparator, and what the change makes possible.
- An optional opening framing sentence ("This proposal is innovative in
  three ways:") earns its place only when enumerating several distinct
  innovations; skip it for a single conceptual or methodological advance.
- Be explicit about *which kind* of innovation is claimed — a new
  method/architecture, or a novel combination or application of
  established tools. The claim must be pitched accordingly:
  - A new *method* must name its conceptual or architectural contribution
    beyond scaling or reapplying an established approach. "More data" is
    not an innovation claim.
  - A new *combination/application* must frame the combination as the
    novel unit — the components stay described as established, and the
    section argues why the combination is new and what it enables.
- A closing risk-mitigation paragraph is optional; include it only when the
  innovations are genuinely high-risk and the mitigation is specific.
  Operational alternatives belong in the Approach section, not here.

Innovation is not novelty for its own sake — the case is that what is new is
also useful, and that the novelty is genuine rather than repackaging
(`SOUL.md` §3).

## Instance calibration (optional)

If the instance's `AGENTS.md` declares a grant-writing calibration index,
load its Innovation note before drafting; it carries that author's
structural preferences. With no calibration bound, these generic defaults
apply — do not present them as any particular author's voice.
