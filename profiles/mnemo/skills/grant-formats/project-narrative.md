# Project Narrative — requirement and drafting defaults

Load when drafting or checking the Project Narrative — and only then.
`section-structure.md` indexes the other section guides.

## The NIH requirement

The Project Narrative attachment (G.220 §8) is required. Per the application
guide, describe the relevance of this research to public health **in, at
most, three sentences** — for example, how the research would contribute to
fundamental knowledge about the nature and behavior of living systems,
and/or the application of that knowledge to enhance health, lengthen life,
and reduce illness and disability. No confidential/proprietary content; no
hyperlinks or URLs unless the NOFO allows them. The NOFO can impose its own
limit.

"At most three" is a ceiling, not a target: a clear, complete
two-sentence Narrative is compliant; a fourth sentence is not. Check
sentence boundaries in the rendered text rather than trusting a count from
an automated splitter — compound sentences are still one sentence.

<https://grants.nih.gov/grants/how-to-apply-application-guide/forms-i/general/g.220-r&r-other-project-information-form.htm>

## Structural defaults (craft, not mandate)

Three sentences carry a natural arc:

1. **The problem** — the disease or scientific challenge in plain language,
   and its health impact.
2. **The approach** — what the proposal will do about it, in plain language.
3. **The payoff** — what the field or the public gains; the translational
   or public-health outcome.

Written for a general audience, not for a scientist: no subfield jargon, no
undefined terms. This is the plainest-language piece of the application.

## Relationship to the Project Summary

The Narrative and the Summary's optional public-health closing cover the
same ground at different depths — but they are separate documents and
should not be copy-pasted. The Summary's closing sits inside its
science-literate 30-line frame; the Narrative is a standalone lay statement
that should be more specific about the public-health payoff. See
`project-summary.md`.

## Instance calibration (optional)

If the instance's `AGENTS.md` declares a grant-writing calibration index,
load its Project Narrative note before drafting; it carries that author's
structural preferences. With no calibration bound, these generic defaults
apply — do not present them as any particular author's voice.
