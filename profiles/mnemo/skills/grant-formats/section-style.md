# Section style — argument criteria for grant sections

This file holds the argument-level criteria for grant sections: how the case
inside each section must be built. It is the companion to
`section-structure.md`, which governs what each paragraph *does*; this file
governs how the argument within it is *constructed*. `STYLE.md` governs the
sentence (voice, rhythm, the machine-writing tells); this file governs the
argument. Neither repeats the other.

`grant-section` consults this file when drafting or revising. `grant-coherence`
consults it during the reviewer read — treating these as strong preferences
that the NOFO and your human's judgment on a specific application can override.

## Evidence base

Unlike `section-structure.md`, whose conventions are corpus-derived (16
ingested grants), the criteria below are session-derived: they were distilled
from the 2026-08-03 critical review and rewrite of a real R01
Specific Aims page (a real instance's Aims page),
one application and one rewrite that your human judged to have worked. They are a
starting hypothesis — the strongest current statement of what a good Aims
page looks like — to be tested and refined on subsequent pages, not yet a
pattern observed across the corpus. When a subsequent Aims page breaks one of
these "rigid" rules for good reason, the rule gets softened, not the page.

## Specific Aims page

The bar: a page a study-section reviewer finds irresistible. Operationally:
the significance is high and unmistakable but not exaggerated; the Aims are
tightly integrated but not interdependent; the prose is clear, tight, and
enjoyable to read. Every word carries its weight. The criteria below are what
that bar decomposes into.

### The significance argument

**Statistics are ballast, not structure.** The significance case must survive
with every number stripped out. Numbers support an argument built on logic;
they do not substitute for one. A page whose opening is a statistics pile —
case counts, hospitalization fractions, fold-increases, prevalence
percentages — reads as an enumeration to a sophisticated reviewer and fails
to persuade, however true every figure is. One precise, well-cited number per
claim; never a pile.

**The pillar architecture.** A strong significance case is carried by several
independent forms of logic. Not every page uses all of them; burden alone
carries none.

- **Structural foreclosure.** The gap is strongest when the target's biology
  forecloses the standard solution, not when the gap is merely large. Argue
  the constraint: what feature of the organism, the disease, or the affected
  population rules out each current approach in turn, so that the reader
  derives the gap from the logic of the problem rather than being told its
  size. When the standard approach is foreclosed by the target itself and the
  alternatives are excluded in the population that carries the burden, the
  gap is a logical consequence — and a consequence is more persuasive than
  any number.
- **Threat trajectory, not snapshot.** Where urgency comes from a worsening
  trend — resistance, incidence, waning durability — argue the dynamics of
  the threat rather than quoting a worst-case point estimate. Why the
  situation keeps getting worse, and why the worsening is built into the
  system: a low-barrier mechanism spreads efficiently once it emerges; the
  region furthest along the trajectory is the future the others are on; and
  the threat concentrates exactly where burden and fewest alternatives
  coincide. This framing is more accurate and more alarming than an outlier
  statistic a reviewer may recognize as cherry-picked.
- **Clinically validated precedent.** The strongest translational argument
  available: the proposed intervention architecture has already been tested,
  in the clinic or in the field. Name the architecture that succeeded and the
  architecture that failed, so the reader can see which category the proposal
  falls in. This converts the proposal from a bet into an instance of a
  validated formula — and naming the unfavorable architecture pre-empts the
  "but similar things have failed" critique before it is made. If a precedent
  exists for the proposed architecture, it belongs on the page.
- **The paradox whose resolution is the knowledge.** Where the biology
  supplies a genuine puzzle — observations that ought not to coexist, like a
  robust response that fails to protect, or universal exposure that leaves
  lifelong susceptibility — the paradox is the most compelling material
  available, because the resolution of the paradox *is* the knowledge the
  grant produces. It also distinguishes the proposal from prior attempts that
  reproduced the problem rather than correcting it. When a paradox exists, it
  earns prominence; burying it behind statistics wastes the page's best
  argument.
- **Disease-surface broadening.** Where the target touches a larger clinical
  surface than the entry-point disease, one sentence naming that broader
  surface expands the impact case meaningfully. The broader claim must be
  qualified to its epistemic status — associative evidence stays associative
  (see calibrated impact below) — but the broadening itself is legitimate: a
  target implicated in a large chronic condition is a bigger public-health
  object than a cause of one acute syndrome.

**Absence is a weak pillar.** "No vaccine exists," "no therapeutic has ever
been reported" — absence-as-significance can close an argument but cannot
carry one. An empty landscape is where the work goes; it is not why the work
matters.

**Precision over punch.** Conflated or imprecise statistics are a live
vulnerability: a careful reviewer will catch that a peak-month fraction was
quoted as an annual figure, or that an unelaborated upper bound of a range
was presented as a finding. Every number on the page should be exactly right,
or not there.

### Aim architecture

**Tightly integrated but not interdependent.** Each Aim should deliver value
on its own terms. A cascade — Aim 3 defined entirely by Aim 1 and Aim 2
outputs, so that one Aim's failure collapses the application — is the
reviewer's kill shot. Integration is real and should be stated; dependence
must be engineered out.

**Independence is stated, not implied.** Name explicitly which components
require no upstream outputs and proceed from day one (a polyclonal mapping
that needs only sera; a platform baseline that needs no epitope maps). One
sentence establishing the parallel architecture pre-empts the interdependence
critique.

**Fallbacks never appear on the Aims page.** Feasibility fallbacks and
alternative strategies belong in the Approach section's alternative-strategies
discussion. Stating a fallback at the Aim level plants feasibility doubt in
the reviewer's mind at exactly the moment the proposal is earning confidence.

**No Aim is a feeder.** An early Aim's terminal experiment is framed as that
Aim's own deliverable, not as a gate into a later Aim. Passive-transfer
efficacy belongs to the Aim that runs it; the mAbs *also* serve as design
templates downstream, not the other direction.

### Page economy

**Approach-level detail stays in the Approach.** Reagent mechanics, platform
brand names, dosing schedules, sort strategies cost space without earning
argument on a one-page document. What survives: what each component
contributes to the logic, and the go/no-go decisions that show scientific
self-awareness (an accessibility milestone reads as rigor, not hedging, when
it names what happens on either branch).

**Complementarity logic over procedure.** Where two platforms serve one Aim,
the page says *why two* — what each sees that the other misses (what natural
infection elicited, versus what lies beyond the imprinted response) — rather
than how each runs. The complementarity is the point of the design; the
procedure is the Approach's job.

### Calibrated impact

**Hypotheses as hypotheses, deliverables as deliverables.** Each Aim opens
with a real, falsifiable hypothesis ("we hypothesize that..."), and the
closing names concrete products. The gap between them is where exaggeration
lives, and it stays closed.

**Evidence qualified to its epistemic status.** Association is stated as
association. Human prevalence plus animal sufficiency is a strong
circumstantial case and should be presented as exactly that — triangulation
strengthens it but does not convert it to causation. A reviewer who catches
an over-standing of the evidence discounts the whole page; the qualification
costs one clause and buys the trust.

**No inflated claims.** "The first human mAbs against this pathogen" is
defensible when true and earned; "will transform the field" is not. The
deliverables, stated plainly, are impressive enough — stating them is the
impact; asserting importance is the tell (`STYLE.md` §4).

### What is rigid vs. flexible

| Element | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Statistics as ballast, not structure | **Rigid** | The argument must survive with every number stripped. |
| At least one logic pillar beyond disease burden | **Rigid** | Structural foreclosure, the precedent, or the paradox. Burden alone is an enumeration. |
| Aim independence stated explicitly | **Rigid** | Cascading failure is the reviewer's kill shot. |
| Fallbacks on the Aims page | **Prohibited** | They live in the Approach's alternative strategies. |
| Approach-level detail on the Aims page | **Prohibited** | Reagent mechanics, dosing, platform specifics. |
| Clinically validated precedent, when one exists | **Strong preference** | Converts a bet into an instance of a validated formula. |
| Paradox framing, when the biology supplies one | **Strong preference** | The resolution of the paradox is the grant's knowledge. |
| Complementarity logic for multi-platform Aims | **Strong preference** | Why two platforms, not how each runs. |
| Epistemic qualification of associative evidence | **Rigid** | One honest clause, never three (`STYLE.md` §4). |

### Prose register

The sentence level is `STYLE.md`'s — its tells (§4) and rhetorical register
(§5) apply in full. One note specific to this page: it is the first page the
reviewer reads, so rhythm and variation earn double weight here. The page
should read as though a person wrote it and enjoyed the argument — formal and
scientific throughout, but never as though a committee approved it.
