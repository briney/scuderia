# Specific Aims argument criteria

Load for drafting, revising, or reviewing the Specific Aims page, alongside
`specific-aims.md`. Other sections use their own guides. `STYLE.md` owns
sentence-level voice; these are generic argument defaults, not evidence of
any particular author's habits. Follow the instance's declared judgments and
section calibration where available; neither can override factual accuracy
or applicable submission requirements.

## Significance

Build the significance case from the scientific problem and the consequence
of solving it. Numbers support that argument rather than replace it. Check
that the case still holds without a list of burden estimates. Use precise,
source-verified statistics; do not conflate annual and peak-period estimates
or present a range endpoint as a representative result.

Use the reasoning the evidence actually supports, without forcing every
proposal into the same pattern:

- **Constraints on existing solutions:** explain which biological or clinical
  feature limits current approaches and why the remaining gap matters.
- **Threat trajectory:** where supported, explain the mechanism of worsening
  resistance, incidence, or durability rather than selecting an alarming
  snapshot. Do not assume one region predicts another without evidence.
- **Clinical or field precedent:** name the intervention that worked and any
  relevant failures. Explain which mechanism and conditions transfer to this
  proposal and which do not; precedent supports feasibility, not guaranteed
  success in another system. A relevant precedent belongs in the Aims case.
- **A genuine biological puzzle:** state the observations to be reconciled
  and the knowledge their resolution would produce. Do not invent a paradox
  where differences in population, assay, or mechanism already explain it.
- **Broader health relevance:** include a supported link to another condition
  where it materially expands the case. Keep associative evidence explicitly
  associative; a larger proposed application is not an established benefit.

“No vaccine exists” or “no therapeutic has been reported” identifies an
absence, but does not alone explain why filling it matters.

## Aim architecture

Each aim should produce something useful independently of downstream use.
State how the aims contribute to the overall question and which components
can proceed without upstream outputs. Avoid an aim defined only as a feeder
into the next. Where dependency is real, do not claim independence: clarify
its effect on the project and put operational alternatives in Approach.

For a hypothesis-testing aim, state a falsifiable hypothesis and a design
that distinguishes it from alternatives. Resource or technology aims may
instead state their objective and success criteria; do not invent a
hypothesis merely to fill a template. Close with concrete products and
expected outcomes, not an unsupported promise to transform the field.

## Page economy

Keep reagent mechanics, dosing schedules, sort strategies, and operational
fallbacks in Approach. On the Aims page, retain the major design choices and
decision criteria needed to understand the scientific logic. When an aim
uses complementary platforms, explain what each can establish that the
other cannot, rather than reproducing both protocols. Keep the project
products visible when shortening background prose.

## Evidence and voice

Label hypotheses, demonstrated findings, expected products, and possible
applications distinctly. Human association plus experimental sufficiency in
an animal model does not establish human causation. Include negative evidence
and the limits of mechanistic analogy where they affect the claim.

Apply `STYLE.md` for prose and the author's actual writing for voice. Use the
optional grant-calibration index declared in the instance's `AGENTS.md` to
load its Specific Aims note; do not treat historical reviewer anecdotes or
funding correlations as universal rules. Keep private examples in the instance.
