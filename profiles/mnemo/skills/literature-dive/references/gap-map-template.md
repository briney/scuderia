## Example (from Alzheimer's vaccine dive)

### 1. Peripheral sink mechanism for vaccine-elicited antibodies

The Bard 2000 paper showed peripherally administered anti-Aβ antibodies
enter the CNS and reduce pathology, but a complementary mechanism — the
"peripheral sink" hypothesis — proposes that antibodies in blood bind
circulating Aβ, lowering the free Aβ concentration and creating an
osmotic gradient that draws Aβ out of the brain. The Zhan review mentions
peripheral monocytes clear ~40-60% of brain-derived Aβ from circulation,
but does not fully develop the sink model.

**Query:** How much Aβ does active vaccination clear peripherally vs
centrally? What is the quantitative contribution of the peripheral sink?

### 2. BBB crossing efficiency of polyclonal vaccine-elicited antibodies

The Zhan review extensively covers engineered passive mAb BBB delivery
(BrainShuttle TfR1 transcytosis), but active vaccines elicit polyclonal
responses — mixed isotypes, varied affinities, potentially different BBB
transport kinetics. The spine review notes immunosenescence constrains
antibody titers but does not address what fraction of vaccine-elicited
IgG actually reaches the brain parenchyma.

**Query:** What percentage of vaccine-elicited anti-Aβ IgG crosses the
BBB? Do polyclonal responses have different CNS penetration than
monoclonal Abs?
